Whisper hears isiXhosa,
but it can't spell it

We played all 2,770 test clips from the NCHLT isiXhosa corpus to stock whisper-small, which has never been taught isiXhosa. As expected, it failed. The useful part is how it failed: it often catches the sounds, but has no idea how isiXhosa is written or which language it is hearing.

Why word errors can go past 100%

WER counts every wrong word, every missing word and every extra word, then divides by the number of words in the true text. isiXhosa words are long, and Whisper chops them into pieces, so it writes more words than were spoken. Across the test set it wrote 15,328 words for 8,107 real ones, about 1.9 for every real word. Here is a real clip from speaker 500:

True textesalikaekhayenilakhe
Whisperesalikaekayenilakia
3 wrong words + 1 extra word = 4 errors ÷ 3 true words = 133% WER · but only 18% CER

By sound, that is close. It split esalika in two, dropped the h in kh, and misheard the ending of lakhe. This is why we report CER next to WER: for isiXhosa, CER shows how close the model is, and WER shows whether the words come out usable.

It mistook isiXhosa for 52 different languages

Whisper's first decision on every clip is which language it is hearing, and isiXhosa isn't one of its options. Its top guess was Swahili, another Bantu language, and those clips came out closest. When it guessed Yoruba or Shona it often got stuck repeating one character over and over, which pushes the letter error above 100%.

It hears the sounds but not the spelling

We counted how often key isiXhosa letter patterns appear in the true text, and how often Whisper wrote them. This count only uses the 1,933 clips where Whisper wrote in the Latin alphabet. It almost never writes kh, hl or dl, and mostly drops the click x. Those patterns are the most distinctive parts of isiXhosa spelling, and they are exactly what fine-tuning has to teach.

What this means for stage 2

Fine-tuning on the NCHLT training set (about 200 other speakers) has three jobs, in the order this baseline suggests: stop Whisper guessing a language and switching scripts, teach it isiXhosa spelling (kh, th, hl, dl, the clicks), and teach it where long words begin and end. The first two should show up quickly in CER, and the third in WER. Stage 2 will be scored on these same 2,770 clips and speakers, so the numbers can be compared directly.
