Adding natural speech fixed long sentences

The stage 2 model scored 3% letter errors on NCHLT's short prompts, but on long, natural sentences it fell apart: it glued words together. We added 13 hours of FLEURS (full news-style sentences) to the training data and changed nothing else. On the hard exam, letter errors fell from 29.8% to 10.2% and word errors from 95% to 44%. The original NCHLT score did not get worse.

What is still wrong

8.8%Letter errors on a typical (median) FLEURS clip. 212 clips are under 5%; 62 clips are at 20% or worse.
1.3%Only 13 of 1,034 long sentences come out perfectly. In a 16-word sentence, one slip is enough to miss.
12.8%Letter errors on the 200 clips whose reference contains digits (9.5% without). FLEURS writes 1990 but speakers say the number in isiXhosa, so part of this is a scoring artefact, the same for every model.
EnglishNames and borrowed English words (Australia, nuclear, Dunlap) are the biggest misses.

How training went, and a lesson

Lesson: the dev check that picks the best checkpoint only contains NCHLT speakers, so on its own it would have said "adding FLEURS made things a bit worse". The two test sets showed the real picture. Next time the dev check should include FLEURS clips too, so it measures what we care about.

What's next

R-0007 is our best model so far. It is saved in your cloud storage at models/R-0007/. Options for the next experiment, one change each:

1Bigger model (whisper-medium, 3× the size) on NCHLT + FLEURS. Usually the biggest single gain. Needs a smoke test first to measure speed and memory; probably 2–3× slower, so roughly $4–6.
2FLEURS in the dev check, so the best checkpoint is chosen on long sentences too. Cheap; it can ride along with option 1.
3More FLEURS weight. It is only 7.5% of the training clips but the harder half of the exam.
4A real isiXhosa tag, audio augmentation, and a comparison with Meta's MMS.
